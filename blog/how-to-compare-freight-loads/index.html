How to Compare Freight Loads on a Load Board

Published October 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Finding a freight listing is the first step. Before you contact a broker or shipper, check whether the load fits your truck, pickup schedule, and next destination. A clear comparison helps you spend less time pursuing shipments you cannot take.

Use this freight load checklist to narrow your options and prepare useful questions before you agree to a shipment.

Start with your truck and your available route

Write down where your truck will be empty, when it will be available, and where you want to go next. Then search for loads that fit those limits.

An origin near your current location is only part of the decision. Check how far you would travel to the pickup and whether the delivery location fits your plans afterward. A route that looks attractive on its own may create extra travel before or after the shipment.

On Golden Route, you can narrow your search by origin, destination, and equipment type. Start with the filters that matter most to your operation, then compare the results that remain. Listings and availability can change; check the board before planning around a specific opportunity.

Confirm the equipment requirements

Do not assume that a load fits your truck because the route works. Review the requested equipment and ask about any shipment details that are missing.

Useful questions include:

  • What equipment type and trailer dimensions does the shipment require?
  • What are the cargo’s weight, dimensions, and loading requirements?
  • Does the shipment require temperature control or particular handling?
  • What equipment will be available at pickup and delivery?

Compare the answers with your actual equipment and operating limits. If a requirement is unclear, resolve it before committing.

Check the pickup and delivery schedule

Look at both ends of the trip. Confirm whether the listed times are appointments, flexible windows, or estimates.

Account for the travel to pickup, time at the facilities, and the commitments you already have. Ask who should be contacted if an appointment changes. A shipment that conflicts with your schedule is not a useful option, even when its destination is appealing.

Compare the whole trip

Use the same information when comparing two loads. Include the miles to pickup, the loaded route, and any additional travel needed to reach your next planned stop.

Then review the offered compensation alongside the costs you expect for that trip. Use your own operating costs rather than a generic earnings estimate. Confirm which expenses or extra services are included in the offer and which need a separate agreement.

If the rate or mileage is missing, treat it as an unanswered question. Do not make a final comparison from incomplete information.

Prepare your questions before making contact

Keep a short note for each load you are considering: origin, destination, equipment, dates, total trip miles, and any unanswered questions. This makes the conversation with the broker or shipper more productive.

Ask for confirmation of the shipment details, offered rate, contact information, and the process for handling changes. Before you accept, make sure the written details match what you discussed.

Use a load board with clear access expectations

Golden Route’s basic access supports searching loads and viewing basic route information. The load board asks users to sign in for load details, and premium access includes rate visibility and broker or shipper contact information.

You can browse Golden Route’s load board to see what is currently listed. If you need rates and direct contact details, review the current plans before choosing an upgrade.

Quick freight load checklist

Before pursuing a load, confirm:

  1. The pickup location fits your truck’s availability.
  2. The delivery location fits your next route.
  3. The equipment and cargo requirements match your capabilities.
  4. The pickup and delivery schedule works for the whole trip.
  5. You have enough rate, mileage, and shipment information to compare it fairly.
  6. The broker or shipper has answered your outstanding questions.

A useful load search ends with a clear decision. Start with the loads that fit your operation, resolve missing details, and contact the broker or shipper with specific questions.
